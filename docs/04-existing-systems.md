# Comparison of Existing Systems

## 1. How to read this document

- **K1 – K10** are the comparison criteria (K = criterion). Each one is described in section 3.
- **FR-xx** is a functional requirement and **NFR-xx** a non-functional requirement from `06-requirements.md`. They show which part of our system answers each criterion.
- In the comparison table, **Yes** means the function is fully supported, **Partly** means it exists in a limited form, **No** means it is absent.

## 2. Selection of systems

No single system was found, in Kazakhstan or abroad, that covers the whole process "screening, finding a specialist, sharing the child's data, plan, sessions and progress". Therefore four systems were selected. Each covers one part of this process and represents one type of existing solution.

| ID | System | Type | Why selected |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | CDC's Milestone Tracker (iOS, Android) | Free developmental milestone app for parents by the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention | Reference solution for parent-completed developmental monitoring |
| B | Kinedu: Baby Development (iOS, Android) | Commercial child development app for parents | Popular app with milestones, progress reports and activities |
| C | DOQ.kz (web, iOS, Android) | Kazakh service for finding doctors and booking with reviews | Local solution for specialist search, ratings and free time slots |
| D | Damumed (web, iOS, Android) | Kazakh medical information system with a patient app | Local system used by polyclinics: appointments, medical card, children's records |

**Scope of comparison for general medical systems (C, D).** DOQ.kz and Damumed cover all of medicine, while this project covers only early intervention. They are included because they are what parents in Kazakhstan actually use today when they look for a specialist or need their child's records. Only the functions relevant to a child with developmental needs are compared: finding a children's specialist, booking, reviews, the child's records available to the parent. The fact that only general-purpose systems are available is itself a finding: there is no dedicated early intervention system for families in Kazakhstan.

## 5. Description of each system

### A. CDC's Milestone Tracker

Free app with illustrated checklists from 2 months to 5 years, tips for supporting development and advice on what to do in case of concern. A milestone summary can be shared with the child's doctor. The app itself states that it does not replace standardised developmental screening tools, and CDC states that it does not collect personal data. Available in English and Spanish.

- **Strength:** evidence-based content, revised in 2022 (see literature review, source S1); clear photos and videos.
- **Gap:** ends with the advice "talk to your doctor"; there is no way to find a specialist or continue working with one.

Sources: https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones-app/ ; https://purl.fdlp.gov/GPO/gpo87203 ; https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/cdc-s-milestone-tracker/1232718688?country=ca

### B. Kinedu

According to the developer, used by over 9 million families. Offers personalised daily activity plans with videos, milestones and progress reports by developmental area, live expert classes and a baby tracker. The free version is limited; the premium subscription costs, for example, $8.99 per month in the US App Store.

- **Strength:** engaging content for parents; progress shown by developmental areas.
- **Gap:** no connection to the child's real specialists; the plan is created by the app, not by a professional.

Sources: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id741277284 ; https://apps.apple.com/app/id741277284

### C. DOQ.kz

Kazakh service that shows doctors' free time slots and allows online booking. Reviews can be written only after a visit, and the doctor's rating takes into account experience, category, academic degree and reviews. In 2024 the service worked with more than 400 private clinics and 3,000 doctors in Kazakhstan and had collected over 340,000 reviews. Free for patients; clinics pay a commission.

- **Strength:** verified reviews and free time slots. Our specialist search follows the same model.
- **Gap:** general medicine and one-time visits; no long-term plan, no child profile, no data sharing between specialists, no screening.

Sources: https://forbes.kz/articles/kak-kazahstanskiy-servis-po-poisku-vrachey-menyaet-rynok-meduslug-i-vyhodit-v-drugie-strany ; https://the-tech.kz/?p=19973 ; https://mwm.ai/apps/doq-kz/1487599921

### D. Damumed

Medical information system used by polyclinics. The patient app (since 2017) has about 20 services: appointments, home calls, medical card, reminders, rating of appointments, a catalogue of partner paid clinics, synchronisation with Apple Health. Parents can see their children's data.

- **Strength:** used by millions of people; parents already see their children's appointments and results in one place.
- **Gap:** general state healthcare; no early intervention plans, goals or home recommendations; the data belongs to the medical institutions, and the parent cannot decide who sees it. Users have reported incorrect records in the app (see section 6).

Sources: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1238801709 ; https://www.weproject.media/articles/detail/damumed-kak-sozdat-it-produkt-kotorym-polzuetsya-3-milliona-kazakhstantsev/ ; https://newtimes.kz/obshchestvo/218584-opasnyi-sboi-v-damumed-poiavliaiutsia-chuzhie-deti-i-analizy

## 6. User feedback

Feedback is summarised in our own words from public sources (app stores, review sites, media). Short complaints without content are not included.

### A. CDC's Milestone Tracker

Rating 4.9 out of 5 (521 ratings, App Store Canada, checked 22.10.2026).

**Advantages mentioned by users:**
- Photos and videos show exactly what each milestone looks like, so parents understand what to look for in their own child.
- Comes from a trusted public health source and is completely free, without ads or subscriptions.
- Some paediatricians download it for their own families and recommend it to patients.

**Disadvantages mentioned by users:**
- Individual reports of the app crashing.
- Only English and Spanish; not usable for most parents in Kazakhstan.
- After a missed milestone the app only advises talking to a doctor; it does not help to find one.

Sources: https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/cdc-s-milestone-tracker/1232718688?country=ca ; https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1232718688/cdc-s-milestone-tracker/reviews ; https://www.cdc.gov/milestonetracker

### B. Kinedu

**Advantages mentioned by users:**
- Gives daily ideas for games and exercises appropriate to the child's age.
- Parents of premature babies find milestone tracking useful for following their child's progress.
- The paid version includes live classes with experts on sleep, parenting and child development.

**Disadvantages mentioned by users:**
- The app constantly offers the paid subscription; some users say it interrupts almost every action.
- Several users report being charged for the annual subscription after cancelling the free trial, and one reports a refused refund for an unused subscription.
- In the free version only a few activities per week are available.

Sources: https://apps.apple.com/app/id741277284 ; https://apps.apple.com/ro/app/id741277284 ; https://apps.apple.com/ch/app/id741277284

### C. DOQ.kz

App Store reviews (Kazakhstan), collected by the author on 23.10.2026. Recent reviews (September 2026) are mostly 5 stars; the critical reviews below are older but describe specific problems.

**Advantages mentioned by users:**
- Booking is convenient and easy to understand; several users say they now book all their appointments only through DOQ.
- The process is fast, and users describe the service as professional.
- On the whole the app works correctly after installation (even users who suggest improvements confirm this).

**Disadvantages mentioned by users:**
- The booking is not always real: one user came to the clinic and was told there was no appointment; another came twice and learned at the reception that the app had cancelled the booking by itself.
- Technical problems: after installation the app got stuck on the "choose a city" screen and nothing loaded.
- Limited search in the app: a user asked to be able to choose not only a doctor but also a clinic and a type of service, as on the website.
- One user felt the app is useful mainly for getting a discount rather than for real booking.

**Relevance for our project:** users value simple and fast booking, but lose trust when a booking in the app does not match reality. In our system a session request is confirmed by the specialist and every change of a session (rescheduling, cancellation) is saved with a reason and shown to the parent (FR-10, FR-20). Search must allow filtering by several parameters (city, specialisation, rating, free time; FR-33, FR-34).

Source: App Store, DOQ.kz, section "Ratings and reviews", checked 23.10.2026.

### D. Damumed

**Advantages mentioned by users:**
- A parent can see the children's records in the app and gets reminders about appointments, vaccinations and test results, so less is forgotten.
- Booking an appointment or a home visit without calling the polyclinic.

**Disadvantages mentioned by users:**
- Users found records of appointments and diagnoses that never happened; the company explained that the app is a tool and records are entered by medical organisations.
- Some users found other people's children attached to their account and other people's test results, and the problem returned even after contacting support.
- Long waiting times for narrow specialists remain, even though booking is online.

Sources: https://tengrinews.kz/kazakhstan_news/medicinskaia-cifrovizaciia-kazaxstana-uspesnyi-opyt-344847/amp/ ; https://newtimes.kz/obshchestvo/218584-opasnyi-sboi-v-damumed-poiavliaiutsia-chuzhie-deti-i-analizy ; https://www.nur.kz/society/1869169-lipovyh-muzej-i-fejkovye-diagnozy-obasnila-gendirektor-damumed/ ; https://www.caravan.kz/?p=662228

## 7. Conclusions

1. **Parent apps (A, B)** help to notice developmental problems but stop there: they do not lead the parent to a real specialist and do not connect to the specialist's work.
2. **Doctor search services (C)** solve "find and book" with trustworthy reviews and free slots, but only for one-time visits in general medicine; users complain when bookings in the app do not match reality.
3. **Medical information systems (D)** store records, but the data belongs to the institutions, there is no early intervention workflow, and users report errors in records, including other people's children attached to their account.
4. **None of the systems gives the parent ownership of the child's profile with consent-based sharing (K4, K5).** This is the main gap. The Damumed problems with wrong records and strangers' children show why the parent's control over the child's data matters.

**What the proposed system takes from them:** evidence-based milestones (A), progress by developmental area (B), verified reviews only after a real visit and free time slots (C), the parent's view of the child's records and reminders (D).

**What it adds:** one chain in one system for Kazakhstan: screening, search of verified early intervention specialists in the parent's city, sharing the parent-owned child profile with consent, and a plan with sessions, attendance and recommendations visible to both sides.

**Limitations of this comparison:** features are taken from official descriptions, app store pages and media; some data may be outdated. User feedback for DOQ.kz was collected directly from the App Store; for the other systems it is summarised from public review pages and media.
